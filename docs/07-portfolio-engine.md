# Portfolio Construction Engine

Version: Draft 1

---

# Purpose

The Portfolio Construction Engine is the core allocation system of Laminar.

Its responsibility is to transform:

* Portfolio Policy
* opportunity rankings
* eligible opportunities
* operational constraints

into a concrete portfolio allocation.

The Portfolio Construction Engine answers:

> How should capital be allocated?

It does not answer:

> Which opportunities are attractive?

That is the responsibility of Opportunity Scoring.

---

# Core Philosophy

The Portfolio Construction Engine exists to allocate capital according to user objectives.

It does not maximize APY blindly.

It does not maximize TVL.

It does not maximize protocol usage.

It optimizes according to the active Portfolio Policy while respecting risk and operational constraints.

---

# Design Principles

## Policy First

The active Portfolio Policy is the primary input.

Portfolio Construction consumes Portfolio Policy, opportunity rankings, and eligible opportunities.

User intent is captured earlier and translated into Portfolio Policy through Normalization and Mapping and profile classification.

Not raw intent inputs.

Not protocol rankings.

Not APYs.

Not incentives.

---

## Risk Aware

Every allocation must incorporate risk constraints.

Higher APY alone is never sufficient justification for allocation.

---

## Diversification By Default

The system should avoid unnecessary concentration.

Diversification is a risk control mechanism.

Not merely a portfolio feature.

---

## Stability Over Churn

Frequent allocation changes are undesirable.

The system should prefer stability when expected benefits are marginal.

---

## Explainability

Every allocation should be explainable.

Users should understand:

* why a protocol was selected
* why a protocol weight was assigned
* why a rebalance was proposed

# Allocation Process

The allocation process consists of several stages.

---

# Stage 1

## Policy Consumption

Portfolio Construction consumes the active Portfolio Policy.

Examples:

```text
Risk Limits

Liquidity Requirements

Target Exposure

Allocation Constraints
```

---

# Stage 2

## Candidate Selection

Remove opportunities that violate:

* risk constraints
* protocol restrictions
* asset restrictions
* liquidity requirements

Remaining opportunities become candidates.

---

# Example

Available:

```text
Morpho

Aave

Moonwell

Aerodrome
```

---

Portfolio Policy filters:

```text
Aerodrome
Risk limit violation
```

---

Result:

```text
Morpho

Aave

Moonwell
```

---

# Stage 3

## Score Ranking

Candidates are ranked by attractiveness.

Example:

```text
Morpho     91

Aave       87

Moonwell   82
```

---

# Stage 4

## Diversification Rules

The engine applies diversification constraints.

---

# Example

Without diversification:

```text
Morpho 100%
```

---

With diversification:

```text
Morpho    50%

Aave      30%

Moonwell  20%
```

---

# Diversification Goals

Reduce:

* protocol concentration
* liquidity concentration
* operational concentration

---

# Stage 5

## Policy Adjustment

Allocations are adjusted according to the active Portfolio Policy.

---

# Conservative User

Example:

```text
policy.selectedProfile = Conservative
policy.riskLimits.minTrustScore = 85
```

Behavior:

* stronger diversification
* stronger trust preference
* higher liquidity preference

---

# Balanced User

Example:

```text
policy.selectedProfile = Balanced
policy.riskLimits.minTrustScore = 75
```

Behavior:

* moderate diversification
* balanced optimization

---

# Return Preference-Oriented User

Example:

```text
policy.selectedProfile = Yield Focused
policy.riskLimits.minTrustScore = 65
```

Behavior:

* higher score concentration
* greater APY sensitivity

# Liquidity Constraints

Portfolio Policy liquidity requirements influence allocation.

---

High Liquidity Requirement

Example:

```text
policy.liquidityRequirements.minLiquidityScore = 85
policy.liquidityRequirements.maxWithdrawalDelay = "instant"
policy.liquidityRequirements.allowLockups = false
```

Behavior:

* prioritize highly liquid protocols
* reduce allocation to lower-liquidity opportunities

---

Lower Liquidity Requirement

Example:

```text
policy.liquidityRequirements.minLiquidityScore = 65
policy.liquidityRequirements.maxWithdrawalDelay = "7 days"
policy.liquidityRequirements.allowLockups = false
```

Behavior:

* allow broader opportunity set

---

# APY Optimization

Yield remains important.

However:

Yield optimization occurs inside constraints.

Not outside them.

---

Priority Order:

```text
Portfolio Policy
 ↓
Risk Limits
 ↓
Liquidity Requirements
 ↓
Return Preference (via Policy)
```

---

This ordering is deliberate.

# Rebalance Thresholds

Small improvements should not trigger reallocations.

---

Example

Current Portfolio Score:

```text
84
```

---

Potential Portfolio Score:

```text
85
```

---

Improvement:

```text
+1
```

---

Likely insufficient.

---

# Hysteresis Principle

Laminar intentionally resists unnecessary movement.

Benefits:

* lower gas costs
* lower churn
* greater portfolio stability

# Non-Goals

The Portfolio Construction Engine does not:

* classify risk events
* calculate protocol metrics
* execute transactions
* manage protocol integrations

---

# Success Criteria

The Portfolio Construction Engine succeeds when:

* allocations align with user intent
* portfolios remain diversified
* risk remains controlled
* yield remains competitive
* rebalances remain justified

---

# Failure Criteria

The Portfolio Construction Engine fails when:

* users receive nearly identical portfolios despite different intent
* concentration becomes excessive
* allocations become difficult to explain
* churn becomes excessive
* gas costs consume portfolio gains

---

# Architectural Principle

The Portfolio Construction Engine is the economic core of Laminar.

It transforms opportunity rankings into actionable capital allocations while preserving user intent, risk discipline, transparency, and operational efficiency.

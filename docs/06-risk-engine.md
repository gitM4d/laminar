# Risk Engine

Version: Draft 1

---

# Purpose

The Risk Engine is responsible for identifying, classifying, evaluating, and monitoring risks across the Laminar ecosystem.

The Risk Engine does not:

- allocate capital
- rank opportunities
- execute transactions
- rebalance portfolios

Its purpose is to provide risk intelligence to other system components.

---

# Core Philosophy

Yield is optional.

Risk is mandatory.

Every portfolio decision must incorporate risk evaluation.

Laminar prioritizes:

- capital preservation
- protocol integrity
- liquidity preservation

before yield optimization.

---

# Design Principles

## Safety Before Yield

If risk increases disproportionately relative to expected yield improvements:

Do not allocate capital.

---

## Deterministic Before Intelligent

V1 relies on deterministic rules.

No AI-based risk decisions.

No black-box models.

No probabilistic autonomous actions.

---

## Explainability

Every risk assessment must be explainable.

Every penalty must be traceable.

Every risk event must be auditable.

---

# Responsibilities

The Risk Engine:

- monitors protocol health
- monitors stablecoin integrity
- consumes liquidity signals
- consumes trust scores
- classifies risk events
- produces risk scores
- generates risk penalties
- emits risk alerts

The Risk Engine consumes Trust Scores and Liquidity Scores generated by their dedicated scoring systems.

It does not calculate Trust Scores or Liquidity Scores.

---

# Risk Domains

---

# Protocol Risk

Measures the risk of interacting with a protocol.

Protocol Trust Score is generated by Trust Scoring.

The Risk Engine consumes Trust Score as an input when applying risk penalties or classifying protocol risk events.

Examples reflected in the consumed Trust Score:

- security incidents
- audits
- protocol age
- TVL

---

Operational risk signals outside Trust Scoring may still be evaluated by the Risk Engine.

Examples:

- governance concerns
- infrastructure degradation

---

# Stablecoin Risk

Measures asset-specific risks.

Examples:

- USDC
- EURC
- DAI

Factors:

- depeg history
- reserve structure
- issuer concentration
- market confidence

---

# Liquidity Risk

Consumes liquidity signals when exit difficulty becomes a risk condition.

Liquidity Score is generated by Liquidity Scoring.

The Risk Engine does not calculate Liquidity Scores.

The following characteristics belong to Liquidity Scoring:

- withdrawal delay
- lockups
- withdrawal queues
- redemption accessibility
- market depth

The Risk Engine may consume these outputs as signals for risk penalties or risk events.

---

# Concentration Risk

Measures excessive exposure.

Examples:

- protocol concentration
- asset concentration
- strategy concentration

---

# Operational Risk

Measures execution-related risks.

Examples:

- protocol pauses
- degraded infrastructure
- oracle failures
- bridge dependencies

---

# Risk Event Classification

Every detected event must be classified.

No event may bypass classification.

---

# Hard Risk Events

Hard Risk Events represent severe threats.

Examples:

- confirmed exploit
- protocol insolvency
- severe stablecoin depeg
- protocol shutdown
- critical oracle failure

---

Characteristics:

- high confidence
- immediate impact
- potentially catastrophic

---

Hard Risk Events may trigger emergency actions.

---

# Soft Risk Events

Soft Risk Events represent deteriorating conditions.

Examples:

- declining liquidity
- TVL deterioration
- APY instability
- elevated utilization
- governance uncertainty

---

Characteristics:

- lower urgency
- gradual impact
- uncertain outcome

---

Soft Risk Events do not trigger emergency actions.

---

# Risk Severity Levels

Every event receives a severity score.

Example:

```text
1 - Informational

2 - Low

3 - Moderate

4 - High

5 - Critical
```

---

Severity scales may evolve over time.

---

# Trust Score Consumption

Trust Scoring generates protocol Trust Scores.

Example:

```text
0
to
100
```

---

Trust Score inputs are consumed by the Risk Engine.

The Risk Engine may use Trust Scores to:

- apply risk penalties
- classify protocol risk events
- explain risk decisions

---

Trust Scores are also consumed by Opportunity Scoring.

---

# Risk Penalty Generation

The Risk Engine converts risk observations into penalties.

Example:

```text
Risk Penalty

0.00
to
1.00
```

---

Higher risk:

Higher penalty.

---

Lower risk:

Lower penalty.

# Emergency Actions

Only Hard Risk Events may trigger emergency actions.

Examples:

- position reduction
- emergency withdrawal
- allocation freeze

---

Emergency actions remain subject to user permissions.

---

# User Notification Requirements

Every significant risk event should be visible.

Examples:

- dashboard alerts
- portfolio warnings
- execution explanations

---

Users should understand:

- what happened
- why it matters
- what Laminar intends to do

---

# Historical Risk Tracking

The Risk Engine should maintain historical records.

Examples:

- previous incidents
- risk score evolution
- protocol risk history

---

V1 may implement minimal history.

Future versions may expand analytics.

---

# Non-Goals

The Risk Engine does not:

- choose allocations
- determine portfolio weights
- calculate APY
- optimize yield
- submit transactions

---

# Success Criteria

The Risk Engine succeeds when:

- catastrophic risks are detected early
- risk penalties improve portfolio outcomes
- users understand risk decisions
- emergency actions are justified and explainable

---

# Failure Criteria

The Risk Engine fails when:

- significant risks go undetected
- excessive false positives occur
- users cannot understand risk actions
- risk signals create unnecessary portfolio churn

---

# Architectural Principle

Risk intelligence should be centralized.

Risk decisions should be explainable.

Risk actions should be constrained.

Risk should influence allocation.

Risk should never be ignored.
